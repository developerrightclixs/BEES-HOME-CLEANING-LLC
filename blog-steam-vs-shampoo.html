Steam cleaning

Why steam cleaning beats shampoo for family homes

Bees Home Cleaning team November 2026 · 6 min read
Share

Carpet shampoo and steam cleaning both promise a fresh floor, but they work in very different ways. If you have kids crawling on the rug or pets napping on it, that difference matters. Here is what really happens to your carpet with each method — and why we choose steam.

How carpet shampoo works

Shampooing scrubs a foaming detergent into the carpet, then tries to pull it back out. The problem is that it never all comes out. The soap left behind is sticky, so it grabs new dirt from shoes and paws. That's why a shampooed carpet can look dirty again within weeks — sometimes faster than before it was cleaned.

How steam cleaning works

Steam cleaning (also called hot water extraction) sprays very hot water deep into the fibers and pulls it straight back out with strong suction. The heat loosens grime, oils and allergens, and the suction carries them away. There's no foam to rinse, so there's no sticky residue waiting to attract dirt.

  • Deeper clean: heat reaches the base of the fibers, where most dirt and allergens hide.
  • No residue: nothing sticky is left behind, so carpets stay cleaner for longer.
  • Fewer chemicals: hot water does most of the work, which is gentler for little hands and paws.
  • Faster drying: strong extraction removes most of the moisture before we leave.

Why it matters for kids and pets

Children and pets spend more time on the floor than anyone else in the house. They breathe in what's in the carpet and touch it all day. Steam removes dust mites, pet dander and pollen instead of trapping them under a layer of soap — a real difference for little ones with allergies or sensitive skin.

“The water we pull out of a ‘clean-looking’ carpet is usually grey. That's the dirt shampoo leaves behind — and steam takes away.”

— Bees Home Cleaning team

What about drying time?

A common worry is that steam leaves carpets soaking wet. With the right equipment it's the opposite. Our dry-out step leaves carpets about 98% dry before we leave, so you can walk on them in socks within a few hours. Open a window or run a fan and they'll be fully dry even sooner.

How often should you steam-clean?

For most family homes, a professional steam clean every 6 to 12 months keeps carpets healthy. If you have pets, young children or someone with allergies, every 3 to 6 months is better for high-traffic rooms. In between, vacuum twice a week and blot spills right away.

The bottom line

Shampoo can make a carpet look clean for a short time. Steam makes it clean — and keeps it that way longer, without residue or harsh chemicals. For a home where kids and pets live on the floor, it's the clear choice.
