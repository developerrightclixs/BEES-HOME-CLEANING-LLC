Steam cleaning

Bring dull grout back to life (and keep it that way)

Bees Home Cleaning team November 2026 · 5 min read
Share

Clean tiles with dark, dingy grout lines still make a floor or shower look dirty. The good news is that most grout isn't stained for good — it's just full of dirt. With the right method, it can look close to new again.

Why grout gets dark

Grout is porous, a bit like a sponge. Every time you mop, dirty water soaks into the lines. In bathrooms, soap scum, body oils and moisture add to it, and mildew can start to grow. Over time, these layers turn light grout grey, brown or even black.

Why scrubbing isn't enough

A brush and cleaner can lift the top layer, but the dirt deep in the pores stays put. Strong chemicals like bleach may lighten the color for a while, but they can wear down grout and don't remove what's trapped inside. That's why scrubbed grout often looks dull again within weeks.

What steam does differently

  • Reaches into the pores: hot, pressurized steam pushes deep into the grout and loosens trapped dirt.
  • Kills mildew and bacteria: high heat sanitizes without harsh chemicals.
  • Protects the surface: no aggressive scrubbing or bleach to wear grout down.
  • Lifts residue: soap scum and grease melt away instead of being smeared around.

“Most of the ‘stained’ grout we see is just dirty. Steam usually brings back the original color in one visit.”

— Bees Home Cleaning team

Two habits that keep grout bright

1. Mop with clean water. Change your mop water often, and finish with a clean-water rinse. Dirty mop water is the number one cause of dark floor grout.

2. Dry the shower. A quick squeegee or towel-dry after the last shower of the day stops moisture, soap and mildew from settling into the lines.

Should you seal your grout?

Yes — once it's properly clean. A grout sealer fills the pores so dirt and water sit on the surface instead of soaking in. It makes everyday cleaning much easier and keeps grout looking fresh for longer. Plan to reseal every one to two years in busy areas.
